Everybody knows about the five room dungeon: Entrance/Guardian, Puzzle/Roleplaying Challenge, Red Herring, Big Boss Battle, Plot Twist, popularized heavily by Johnn Four in the early 2000s. Cool shit, but i feel we can make it tighter. This is an exercise in efficiency, not practicality. but who knows, maybe we'll get there.

now, why is the reason I've done this? In SourceMaster_'s gemology episode on The Test (s1e3) in Steven Universe, he mentions a three room dungeon like its something that everybody knows about, as if the same level of efficacy as the Five Room Dungeon. I spent *hours* trying to find this thing and couldn't find anything except a supposedly existent dnd 4e three room dungeon made by Chris Tulach, which i also could not find. Ass. So in an effort to keep any further disasters like this from happening, here we go.

Okay, so there are actually a couple types of three room dungeon. In the Tulach Configuration, supposedly there is a room that is all skill checks, a room that is all combat, and then a big boss battle room. In the McDowall Configuration, the first room is an introduction to a concept, then a reversal or twist on that concept, then a final twist on that concept. Introduction to a monster sort of thing, a very Nintendo thing. Finally, the Acrisian Configuration in which there is a room that is a room full of traps, a room full of combat, and then a room full of treasures typically hidden or locked off by puzzles and traps.

Regardless of which configuration is taken, a constant theme should be kept: it could be slime, it could be computers, but it should be felt throughout the dungeon entirely. Entries from the Onegeon Jam on itch.io recently could serve for a pretty good jumping-off point for doing a three room dungeon (hereby the TRD). Anywho, lets get hands on with a few examples of each type of TRD.

## TULACH CONFIG

So enter the dungeon and room 1 is a massive chasm. Truly ridiculously large chasm spanning around thirty feet straight across. There are walls on either side, so you could wallrun if you're a monk or equivalent, but dropping down is probably death OR!!! drops you into a second layer of the dungeon. There's a small pebble floating in the center of the chasm, which is weird. Further exploration tells us that there is actually an invisible path that leads across the chasm. Skill checks to get across, either by spray paint or glow bugs or cat sixth sense or something. 

Room 2 looks to be a visible straight path across another huge chasm, but this time there are stone golems or like skeleton archers standing on invisible platforms, hovering above the dark abyss. They shoot at you. A note to GMs, you can make your archers a lot funnier by poisoning their arrows, rolling for effect on a hit. Make sure to tick damage down a bit though, a huge amount of damage *and* confusion effects can get stupid. Alternatively, a bunch of wizards or kobold shamans or whatever with push and pull spells to shove players off the path. **When you make a battle room, make the battlefield itself interactable. You should be able to do stuff with the battlefield**. You can probably make this more fun by sticking buttons or levers on a wall, some of which dump enemies into the abyss, some of which remove blocks from the bridge your compatriots walk on.

Room 3 is a final big room, seemingly empty-- but wait! there are walls! Alas, an enormous labyrinth, and a levitating wizard or flying dragon or something soaring overhead and raining havoc down on the party. Have to figure out where the walls are and *also* dodge ranged attacks and the big boss probably has traps or other such bullshit in the labyrinth because they're like that. But beat the guy and you get his glasses that let you see invisible shit and bing bang boom you're fine and dandy and can plunder his treasure.

## MCDOWALL CONFIG
Room one: black pudding in a room full of electrical wires (im imagining something like the Positronic Library by Yochai Gal), wandering into a pile of diodes that shocks it with a trillion volts. Players might note that it does not perish, but splits into two, smaller black puddings which then wander toward the players. They might also notice that the metal diodes are dissolved and corroded and broken and fucked. 

Room two: black pudding crawling around on the ceiling, with a lit candelabra also hanging on the ceiling. Black pudding crawls too close to it, gets set on fire, and is now a flaming black pudding (this isn't orthodox but i dont care. the black pudding is a black viscous fluid which to me means it should be extra flammable and does fire damage. fight me.). Flops off the ceiling, players fight a flaming black pudding in a room with a bunch of gasoline puddles and explosive barrels or something.

Room three: wooden room with a lot of exposed wiring hanging just over the floor. Also a big black pudding nest. black puddings crawl around and maybe get shocked and split into more black puddings, prolonged fighting might get electricity to arc onto the floor and start a fire, where black puddings will quickly spread it, bad overall.

Room one is a dark hallway with a bunch of crushers that thump rhythmically with a pneumatic hiss that puts out torches and blows away dancing lights. Getting through without the lights on is very difficult, slightly less so when you do. Find either a non-fire related light source or just get your way through. A lever at the end turns off the crushers.

Room two is a big open arena with a bunch of crushers with accompanying pressure plates, with a goblin or something controlling the stompy action. Roll a die every round to see which crushers stamp down and which ones stay up. Maybe if too many orcs get turned into mush on a single crusher, the mechanism stops working and it can't go back up. Put a bunch of ranged fighters in there as well as the battlefield gets more and more claustrophobic to play with the crushers as cover. 

Room three is a two part room, with a small treasure chest in the open, and big one in a stylized oyster. There's a big clock that says ten seconds on it that continuously ticks down when someone opens the chest or steps into the oyster. When it hits zero, the thing slams shut, kills whoever's inside, and locks the treasure within forever. the clock doesn't reset either, so it's continuously going down. put some big loot there as well that's locked to the chest or stuck in another way that burns time.
